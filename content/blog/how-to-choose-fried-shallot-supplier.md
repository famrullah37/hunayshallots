Fried shallots look simple, but quality varies a lot between suppliers. Two products can look the same in a photo and taste very different after three months on a shelf.

If you import fried shallots for retail, food service or manufacturing, use this checklist before you sign a supply deal.

## 1. Raw Material

Ask which shallot variety is used and where it is grown.

- Shallots with **lower water content** fry crisper and keep their crunch longer.
- A supplier that knows its variety and growing region usually has better control over quality.

## 2. Ingredients

Read the ingredient list carefully.

- **Best:** shallots, oil, salt.
- **Watch out for:** flour, starch or fillers. They add weight and lower the cost, but also lower the quality.
- **Ask about preservatives.** Many buyers today prefer clean labels.

## 3. Type of Oil

The frying oil affects taste and shelf life. Ask which oil is used and how often it is changed. Fresh oil gives a cleaner taste and a lighter color.

## 4. Certifications

Certificates show that the supplier follows food safety systems. Common ones include:

- **Halal** — required by many buyers in Asia, the Middle East and Muslim communities worldwide.
- **HACCP** — food safety hazard control.
- **GMP** — good manufacturing practice.
- **ISO 9001** — quality management.
- **National food registration** — in Indonesia, this is BPOM.

Ask for copies, and check that names and dates are valid.

## 5. Shelf Life and Packaging

Fried shallots are sensitive to moisture.

- Ask for the **shelf life unopened** and how it was tested.
- Check the **packaging type** — vacuum packs or sealed jars protect crispness better.
- Ask about **bulk packaging** and carton sizes for shipping.

## 6. Minimum Order and Capacity

Make sure the supplier fits your volume.

- What is the **minimum order quantity (MOQ)**?
- What is the **monthly production capacity**?
- Can they handle seasonal peaks, such as holidays?

## 7. Samples

Never order in bulk without samples. When you test them:

- Check color, crunch, oil and salt level.
- Leave one opened pack for a week and check it again.
- Compare samples from more than one supplier side by side.

## 8. Private Label Options

If you want to sell under your own brand, ask if the supplier offers **private label (OEM)** production — your packaging, your label, their product.

## 9. Export Experience and Documents

A supplier that already exports knows the paperwork. Ask:

- Which countries they ship to now.
- Which documents they provide (certificates, health or origin documents).
- Which **Incoterms** they offer, such as FOB or CIF.

## 10. Communication

Fast, clear replies in English save time and avoid costly mistakes. Pay attention to how quickly and clearly the supplier answers your questions during the quote stage.

## Quick Checklist

- Variety and origin of shallots
- Ingredients: shallots, oil, salt only
- Certifications with valid copies
- Shelf life and packaging
- MOQ and capacity
- Samples tested side by side
- Private label option
- Export documents and Incoterms
- Clear communication
