**Fried garlic** is thinly sliced or chopped garlic fried in oil until golden and crisp. It is a common topping across Asia — in Indonesia it is called *bawang putih goreng*, in Thailand *kratiam jiao*, and in Japan it is a popular ramen topping often sold as "garlic chips".

Compared with fresh garlic, it is ready to use, shelf-stable, and adds both **flavor and crunch** in one step.

## What Does Fried Garlic Taste Like?

Good fried garlic is **nutty, savory and slightly sweet**, with none of the sharp bite of raw garlic. Frying turns that harshness into a mellow, roasted flavor.

The color matters. Light golden garlic tastes sweet and toasty. Dark brown garlic tastes **bitter** — a sign it was fried too long or at too high a temperature.

## Fried Garlic vs. Garlic Powder vs. Fresh Garlic

| | Fried Garlic | Garlic Powder | Fresh Garlic |
|---|---|---|---|
| Texture | Crispy | Fine powder | Soft, juicy |
| Flavor | Roasted, nutty | Flat, mild | Sharp, pungent |
| Prep needed | None | None | Peel and chop |
| Best use | Topping, finishing | Rubs, seasoning blends | Cooking base |

Fried garlic is a **finishing ingredient**. It shines when added at the end, so the crunch stays.

## How to Use Fried Garlic

- **Ramen and noodle soups** — sprinkle on just before serving
- **Fried rice** — mix half in at the end of cooking, use the rest on top
- **Rice porridge (congee, bubur)** — classic with fried shallots and spring onion
- **Stir-fried vegetables** — toss in for crunch, especially with greens like kangkung or bok choy
- **Steak and grilled meat** — a popular garlic chip topping in Japan and Korea
- **Salads and rice bowls** — adds texture without extra prep
- **Garlic oil** — crush fried garlic into warm oil for a quick flavored drizzle

Tip: combine fried garlic with [fried shallots](/blog/what-are-fried-shallots). Shallots bring sweetness, garlic brings depth — together they make almost any bowl taste finished.

## How to Judge Quality When Buying in Bulk

Whether you are a restaurant, distributor or food manufacturer, check these points before committing to a supplier:

1. **Color.** Even, light-to-medium golden. Many dark or burnt pieces mean bitterness.
2. **Crispness.** It should snap, not bend. Soft garlic has absorbed moisture or was not fried long enough.
3. **Oil level.** Pieces should not leave a heavy oily film on paper.
4. **Ingredients.** Ideally only garlic, oil and salt. Watch for flour, starch or flavor enhancers added as filler.
5. **Smell.** Roasted and clean. A stale or rancid smell means old oil or old stock.
6. **Packaging.** Moisture-barrier pouches or vacuum packs keep it crisp much longer than thin plastic bags.
7. **Certificates.** For export and food service, ask for Halal, HACCP or ISO documents.

## How to Store Fried Garlic

- Keep it **sealed, cool and dry**.
- Do not refrigerate opened packs — fridge humidity makes it soft.
- Use a dry spoon every time.
- Unopened vacuum packs typically keep **6–12 months**.

If it gets slightly soft, warm it in a dry pan over low heat for a minute to bring back the crunch.
