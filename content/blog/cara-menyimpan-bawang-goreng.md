Fried shallots are an indispensable garnish in Indonesian cooking. From nasi goreng to soto, chicken porridge to noodles — a sprinkle of crispy fried shallots is always the finishing touch that makes a dish feel complete.

But many people find their fried shallots go soft quickly and lose their crunch. With the right storage method, quality fried shallots can stay crispy for months.

## 1. Make Sure Fried Shallots Are Completely Cool

Never seal or transfer fried shallots to a closed container while still warm. Trapped steam will condense into moisture and make the shallots soggy.

**Tip:** After frying, or after opening a package, leave the fried shallots in an open container for 10–15 minutes until they reach room temperature before storing.

## 2. Use an Airtight Container

Contact with humid air is the main enemy of fried shallot crispiness. Store them in:

- Glass jars with tight rubber seals
- Food-grade plastic containers with locking lids
- Zip-lock bags with the air squeezed out

## 3. Keep Away from the Stove and Direct Sunlight

Fried shallots are very sensitive to heat and light. **Best spot:** A dark, cool kitchen cabinet. Ideal storage temperature is below 25°C.

## 4. Do Not Store in the Refrigerator

Refrigerators have high humidity that will actually make fried shallots go soft faster.

**Exception:** In very hot and humid climates, they can be stored in the freezer in an airtight container.

## 5. Always Use a Dry Spoon

A single drop of water or one damp spoon is enough to ruin the crispiness of the surrounding fried shallots.

## How Long Do Fried Shallots Last?

| Condition | Shelf Life |
|---|---|
| Unopened vacuum pack | 6–12 months |
| After opening (stored correctly) | 1–2 months |
| Stored carelessly | 1–2 weeks |
